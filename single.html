AI-RAN Sustainability and Community-Centered Emerging Technologies

My work connects telecommunications engineering, B5G research, artificial intelligence and practical education. I am especially interested in how shared AI-and-RAN infrastructure can be evaluated not only for performance, but also for energy use, carbon impact and social value.

The research theme AI-RAN Sustainability Accounting: Energy and Carbon Trade-Offs in Shared AI-and-RAN Infrastructure, co-authored with Imoter Igbazenda Tijah, examines how emerging network intelligence can be planned with sustainability metrics from the beginning.

From B5G Research to Local Capacity Building

My telecommunications research includes 5G network slicing, resource scheduling, traffic anomaly detection and Internet of Vehicles simulations using NS-3, MATLAB and Python. The same engineering mindset also shapes my teaching at PASS-DRC Academy, where students learn AI fundamentals, Python and introductory robotics through practical local problem-solving.

PASS-DRC Academy and the Elima Programme are built around a simple conviction: emerging technology education should reach learners even where access to computers has historically been limited. Bilingual curricula in French and Swahili make AI and robotics more approachable for students preparing to participate in the digital economy.

Across research, teaching and community service, my goal is to translate advanced communication systems into tools, skills and evidence that help communities prepare for the next generation of connectivity.
